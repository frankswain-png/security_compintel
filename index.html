CrowdStrike Active

FalconFlank is a privilege-escalation proof-of-concept against the CrowdStrike Falcon sensor for Windows, released publicly on 3 September by a researcher operating variously as Nightmare Eclipse, Chaotic Eclipse and MSNightmare. The technique targets Falcon's remediation routine for malicious Microsoft Office macros: because the cleanup runs with elevated privileges, an attacker who can place controlled files in its path can have that routine act on them unsafely and spawn a command prompt running as SYSTEM. Reporting confirms it works against fully patched Windows 11 25H2 and Windows Server 2025 hosts with the macro-removal feature enabled. As of the close of the window CrowdStrike had not assigned a CVE, had not published a CVSS score and had not shipped a fix, directing customers instead to disable the "Microsoft Office File Suspicious Macro Removal" Windows policy setting while retaining Cloud Anti-malware for Microsoft Office Files. No in-the-wild exploitation had been identified.

The second-order read is about disclosure posture rather than the bug itself. FalconFlank arrived as one of three PoCs the same researcher dropped in a short span — alongside PrettyPrague against the Avast sandbox and GreenSection against a shared NVIDIA user-mode memory section. Gen Digital has closed the Avast issue; NVIDIA is investigating; CrowdStrike is alone in still running on a customer-side configuration workaround. That comparison is the part buyers will notice, and it lands nine days after CrowdStrike closed a conference built on the claim that its agent is the trusted control point for AI-era security. Note also that the mitigation is a capability downgrade, not a neutral toggle: customers who apply it lose a remediation feature for the duration.

Watch signal: Elastic Security should expect this to surface in competitive conversations as an argument about agent blast radius — the more security functions collapse into a single privileged endpoint agent, the more a flaw in that agent's remediation logic becomes an enterprise-wide escalation path. The credible Elastic position is architectural rather than opportunistic: open detection content and behavioural coverage for EDR-abuse and living-off-the-security-tool techniques, plus the ability to ingest and correlate endpoint telemetry independently of the agent vendor. Field teams should be briefed to discuss the class of problem, not to campaign on a competitor's unpatched vulnerability.

Standing items: Fal.Con 2026 ran 31 August to 3 September at Mandalay Bay under the theme "Securing the AI Revolution," producing more than a dozen announcements. Directly SIEM-relevant: CrowdStrike unveiled the next evolution of its agentic SOC, claiming the first coordinated multi-agent investigations spanning endpoint, identity, SaaS, cloud and network simultaneously, with Falcon Next-Gen SIEM positioned as the unifying layer for AI-ready data, agentic investigations and governed automation. Platform-level: Falcon Guardian, an AI detection and response product covering data, models, prompts, agents, identities and infrastructure at runtime, launched 1 September and feeds AI-agent telemetry into Falcon Next-Gen SIEM as first-party data; it is being extended through Google Agent Gateway to cover Google Cloud enterprise AI applications. CrowdStrike also announced SafeMind, a family of security models and harnesses from its Cyber Superintelligence Lab built with NVIDIA Nemotron, pairing an offensive model that finds an attack path with a defensive model that closes it. Open question: whether a CVE and sensor update for FalconFlank land before the mitigation guidance becomes a support burden.

Palo Alto Networks Quiet

Nothing new from Palo Alto Networks inside the window, but only just — on 1 September 2026, four days before it opened, the company announced it had acquired Console, an AI-native platform for agentic workflows, with financial terms undisclosed. Console is being folded into Cortex to deepen autonomous investigation, signal prioritisation and action, which puts it squarely in the same contested ground as CrowdStrike's agentic SOC push and Google's Threat Hunt Agent. It follows a heavy year of consolidation: the $3.35 billion Chronosphere acquisition closed 29 January 2026, giving Cortex XSIAM an observability-grade telemetry pipeline — Chronosphere reported over $160 million ARR at end-September 2025 — and the $25 billion CyberArk acquisition closed 11 February 2026, adding identity security to the platform. The strategic read is that Palo Alto is assembling XSIAM's data layer, identity layer and agent layer through purchase rather than build, which shortens its roadmap but leaves integration risk visible to buyers. Research caution: Cortex XSIAM 3.0 coverage is dated 28 April 2025 and reads as current in search results; confirm the year before treating any XSIAM version announcement as new.

Two dated deprecations land imminently and are the nearest catalyst in this edition: on 14 September 2026 the legacy Azure Data Collection API is deprecated, and the containerized SAP data connector agent is permanently disabled and stops delivering SAP logs to Sentinel. The August update added UEBA anomaly insights with explainable context to the Behaviors layer and extended coverage across Fortinet, AWS GuardDuty, Check Point and Zscaler, while Sentinel data connectors for Auth0, CrowdStrike Falcon and Salesforce Service Cloud gained multi-account ingestion through the Codeless Connector Framework. Research caution: the Sentinel data lake reached general availability on 30 September 2025 alongside the Sentinel graph and MCP server public previews; that announcement matches September queries almost perfectly and must not be read as 2026 news.

Google SecOps Quiet

No Google SecOps announcements in the window; the most recent "What's New in Google SecOps" community updates are dated 10 and 17 August 2026. The significant recent capability is the Threat Hunt Agent, in public preview for Enterprise Plus customers since early August — a Gemini-powered agent that runs autonomous hunts seeded from Google Threat Intelligence objects such as an actor, campaign, malware family, toolkit or TTP, executing up to 30 steps over one to two hours and opening a case with its findings, with Mandiant frontline expertise and MITRE ATT&CK as the reasoning frame. Two dated migration deadlines are worth tracking: Stage 2 of the SOAR migration to Google Cloud was extended from 30 September to 30 November 2026, and the MANDIANT_ACTIVE_BREACH_IOC, MANDIANT_FUSION_IOC and OPEN_SOURCE_INTEL_IOC feeds are deprecated ahead of removal on 18 March 2027, with customers directed to the consolidated GTI_IOC feed. The strategic direction is consistent — fold Mandiant intelligence into the platform as agent-consumable context rather than as a separate subscription.
