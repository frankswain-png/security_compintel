TLDR

This window belongs to two vendors. CrowdStrike's Fal.Con 2026 keynote stack — Project QuiltWorks third-party ingest, Falcon Onum pipeline economics, Falcon on Google Cloud and Snowflake, SafeMind frontier models with NVIDIA — is a coordinated push to neutralize the openness and cost objections Elastic raises against Falcon Next-Gen SIEM. Palo Alto Networks paired a blowout Q4 FY26 (NGS ARR $9.10B, +63%) with the Console acquisition and the notable disclosure that half its new observability logos now attach XSIAM. SentinelOne, Microsoft, Splunk/Cisco and Google SecOps were all quiet. The through-line is agentic AI plus data-pipeline cost control as the new SIEM battleground — and both active vendors are now competing on Elastic's home turf of open ingest and data economics rather than ceding it.

Vendor details
CrowdStrike Active

Fal.Con 2026 (Aug 31 – Sep 3, Mandalay Bay, ~10,000 attendees) landed squarely in the window and produced the densest news of any vendor.

Directly SIEM-relevant: Project QuiltWorks expanded to pipe real-time telemetry from 12 third-party vendors — Zscaler, Netskope, ExtraHop, Rubrik, Abnormal AI, Picus, SafeBreach, AttackIQ, HackerOne, Horizon3, Artemis, Terra — into Falcon Next-Gen SIEM, with Falcon Onum pipelines claimed to cut storage costs up to 50%. SailPoint pushed identity governance data into Next-Gen SIEM.

Platform-level: Falcon now runs on Google Cloud infrastructure and is listed on Snowflake Marketplace, drawing down against pre-committed Snowflake capacity. CrowdStrike also launched Falcon IQ (50+ AI agents), Falcon Guardian for AI-agent runtime security, a Cyber Superintelligence Lab, and SafeMind — frontier security models (Red Tempest offensive, Blue Solano defensive) built with NVIDIA Nemotron. The Optiv partnership crossed $2B total contract value.

CrowdStrike is attacking Elastic's core differentiators simultaneously — open third-party data ingest (QuiltWorks), cost-per-GB economics (Onum pipeline, "50% storage reduction"), and data-residency/BYO-cloud flexibility (Google Cloud plus Snowflake) — eroding the "open, cheap, bring-your-own-data SIEM" argument Elastic has used against Falcon NG-SIEM's closed-ecosystem reputation.

Palo Alto Networks Active

On September 1 PANW reported Q4/FY26 results: revenue $3.41B, up 34% year over year; Next-Gen Security ARR $9.10B, up 63%; nearly $1B in net-new NGS ARR in a single quarter. It disclosed that 50% of observability net-new logos in 2H FY26 included an XSIAM cross-sell.

The same day it announced the acquisition of Console, an AI-native agentic platform (terms undisclosed), to embed natural-language-driven agentic workflows into Cortex.

The XSIAM-plus-observability cross-sell datapoint is the sharpest competitive signal here — PANW is now explicitly landing observability deals and attaching SIEM, a direct assault on Elastic's own search-platform-spanning-security-and-observability motion.
