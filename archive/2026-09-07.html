Vendor detail

CrowdStrike — Falcon, Next-Gen SIEM

Active

Fal.Con 2026 took over Mandalay Bay on 1–2 September and produced the densest announcement block of the window. On 1 September CrowdStrike established the Cyber Superintelligence Lab, positioned as a frontier AI research organisation for cyberdefence and AI safety, uniting its AI researchers, offensive operators and incident responders on Falcon platform data and adversary intelligence. On 2 September it expanded its OpenAI partnership on two fronts: securing OpenAI Codex agents with Falcon Guardian, and bringing OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Cyber onto the Falcon platform starting with the Frontier AI Readiness and Resilience (FAIRR) service. Customer and partner awards the same day named NVIDIA, United Airlines, Salesforce and AWS among recipients.

Directly SIEM-relevant: the next evolution of the Agentic SOC delivers coordinated multi-agent investigations running simultaneously across endpoint, identity, SaaS, cloud and network, including threats against enterprise AI systems — model abuse, prompt injection and exfiltration through AI assistants. The enabling piece is a shared context layer built on Enterprise Graph, so every agent works from one memory of the environment. Certified data pipelines built on Falcon Onum streaming technology filter noise at ingestion and run detection inside the pipeline, which CrowdStrike says can reduce data storage costs by up to 50%. Falcon Next-Gen SIEM added ingest from Abnormal AI, Artemis Security, AttackIQ, ExtraHop, HackerOne, Horizon3, Netskope, Picus Security, Rubrik, SafeBreach, Terra Security and Zscaler, and third-party agents can now reach Falcon tools through a CrowdStrike-managed MCP server. Platform-level: the Agentic Identity Provider gives each AI agent a trusted identity tied to the humans and workloads behind it with just-in-time least-privilege access, positioning Falcon Next-Gen Identity Security as the identity control plane for the agentic enterprise. Real-Time Supply Chain Attack Protection intercepts npm install and pip install transactions across npm and PyPI on Windows, macOS and Linux, blocking malicious packages before embedded code runs — and extends to every endpoint where agentic applications run, not only developer workstations. The Falcon platform was also brought to the Anthropic Claude marketplace.

Watch signal: two of these land squarely on Elastic Security's competitive axes. The Onum-based certified pipelines turn ingest-cost control into a CrowdStrike feature rather than an Elastic differentiator, and the managed MCP server plus the broadened third-party connector list attacks the openness argument Elastic has used against Falcon's historically endpoint-anchored data model. Expect both to appear in Next-Gen SIEM displacement conversations this quarter; the counter-position is total cost at retention scale and the absence of a proprietary agent requirement, not feature parity on agents.

Standing items: CrowdStrike presents at the Citi Global TMT Conference and the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, both on 10 September 2026 — watch for Next-Gen SIEM ARR disclosure and any Onum attach-rate colour. Out of window for context: Falcon Next-Gen SIEM support for Microsoft Defender for Endpoint was announced on 22 March 2026, which remains the clearest signal of CrowdStrike's willingness to ingest a rival's endpoint telemetry. Open question: whether certified pipelines are licensed separately from Next-Gen SIEM ingest or bundled, since that determines how real the 50% storage claim is in a competitive TCO model.

Palo Alto Networks — Cortex XSIAM / XDR

Active

On 1 September 2026 Palo Alto Networks announced it had acquired Console, described as an AI-native platform that lets AI agents carry out operational tasks. Financial terms were not disclosed. Console's background is in automating IT operations — access changes, account provisioning, employee offboarding — with more recent expansion into security workflows. Palo Alto said the technology will become part of Cortex, where it is intended to help security teams investigate activity, prioritise work and take action across their environments, with named applications in alert investigation, prioritisation and incident handling.

The second-order read is about sequencing rather than technology. Cortex XSIAM already markets autonomous security operations; what Console supplies is the execution layer — the connective tissue that turns a decision into a change in a production system — and Palo Alto has chosen to buy it rather than build it. That compresses the timeline for XSIAM moving from "recommends an action" to "performs the action", and it arrived in the same 48 hours as CrowdStrike's multi-agent Agentic SOC and Splunk's AI SOC Analyst. Three of the six vendors tracked here moved on autonomous execution in a single window, which is a category shift rather than three coincidences.

Watch signal: the competitive question in Elastic Security evaluations is shifting from detection quality to what an agent is permitted to do unsupervised and who is accountable when it acts wrongly. Elastic's position should lead on transparency of agent reasoning, auditability of automated actions and customer control over approval boundaries — areas where an acquired IT-automation stack bolted into Cortex is likely to be weakest in its first release cycles.

Standing items: no purchase price, close date or product timeline has been disclosed, so the practical question is when Console capability actually appears in a Cortex release rather than a roadmap slide. No Cortex XSIAM product release news landed in this window separately from the acquisition. Research caution: Palo Alto's original XSIAM launch (February 2022) and its general-availability announcement later that year rank highly in search and read as current — verify the year on any XSIAM "launch" or "next-generation SOC" headline.

Microsoft — Sentinel / Defender XDR

Quiet

Nothing verifiable in the 1–4 September window. The standing story remains the consolidation of Microsoft's security estate into the Defender portal: managing Microsoft Sentinel in the Azure portal was originally set to retire on 1 July 2026, and Microsoft extended that sunset to 31 March 2027 after customer feedback. Sentinel continues as a full SIEM service with operations transitioning into the Defender portal. In parallel, Microsoft Defender Threat Intelligence is converging into Defender XDR and Sentinel — delivered inside the unified SecOps experience at no additional cost — with the standalone MDTI product retired on 1 August 2026. On the data side, the Sentinel data lake reached general availability with Sentinel graph and a Sentinel MCP server in public preview, offering more than 350 native connectors and retention priced below 15% of traditional analytics logs, alongside data federation through Microsoft Fabric that analyses security data in place, plus native filtering and splitting in the Defender portal to route data between analytics and lake tiers before ingestion. That pricing tier, not any product launch, is the live competitive pressure on Elastic Security. Research caution for this vendor: Microsoft Ignite and RSAC announcements recur annually and Sentinel data lake material from 2025 resurfaces prominently in searches for current news — the July and September 2025 Sentinel data lake posts are the most common false positives. Splunk (Cisco) — Enterprise Security

Active

Splunk released Enterprise Security 8.7.0 on 2 September 2026. The centrepiece is the AI SOC Analyst, which takes investigation and response-plan context and automatically executes permitted actions, recommends next steps where analyst approval is required, and completes response tasks — moving Splunk from investigation to response without a handoff. Two further agents ship alongside it: a Detection Builder Agent with improved reliability and accuracy when creating and refining detections, including better SPL guidance and validation for Essentials Edition, and a Connector Builder Agent that generates, tests and refines connectors to widen automation coverage across more tools. The release also adds MCP access to investigation context, a reworked navigation experience with customisable layout and icons, a streamlined analyst queue with team-based queues and saved views, and deeper change history in activity logs.

The Connector Builder Agent deserves separate attention from the AI SOC Analyst. Connector coverage has been the slow, unglamorous constraint on every SIEM's automation reach, and generating connectors with an agent attacks the integration moat that content-library breadth has traditionally provided. If it works as described, the defensible differentiator moves from how many integrations a vendor ships to how fast a customer can create the one they need — which is a materially different competitive game and one that favours whoever has the better generation loop rather than the bigger catalogue.

Watch signal: "permitted actions, executed automatically" is now the phrase to expect from Splunk in competitive evaluations, and Elastic Security needs a precise answer on where its own automation sits on the approval spectrum. The MCP access to investigation context also matters — Splunk, CrowdStrike and Microsoft have all now exposed investigation context over MCP, so agent interoperability is becoming table stakes rather than a differentiator.

Standing items: 8.7.0 is a point release only ten weeks or so after 8.6, so the release cadence itself is worth tracking as a signal of Cisco-era investment levels in Enterprise Security. Open question: whether the AI SOC Analyst is gated to a particular Enterprise Security edition, which determines how much of the installed base actually gets it. Research caution: Splunk .conf recurs annually and Cisco's September 2025 agentic-AI SOC announcement ranks highly in searches for current Splunk security news — confirm the year before treating any Cisco or Splunk SOC-automation headline as new.

Google SecOps — Chronicle / Mandiant

Quiet

No product announcement in the 1–4 September window, though one commercial change took effect inside it: consumption of purchased Google SecOps Security Tokens, originally due to begin on 1 July 2026, was delayed and started on 1 September 2026 — a billing-model change rather than news, but one that will surface in customer conversations this month. The most recent product substance is from August 2026, when Google launched the Threat Hunt Agent in public preview for Enterprise Plus customers: a Gemini-powered agent that autonomously runs proactive hunts using Google Threat Intelligence and Mandiant expertise, opening a case in Case Management, typically taking 60–90 minutes per run and limited during preview to two concurrent hunts and five per day, launched against predefined Google TI objects such as an actor, campaign, malware family, toolkit or TTP. Also in August, Google SecOps SIEM added a public preview for analysing feed activity with Cloud Logging to debug ingestion pipelines and diagnose log-delivery issues. Two dated migration items are worth holding: Stage 2 of the SOAR migration to Google Cloud was extended from 30 September to 30 November 2026, and the MANDIANT_ACTIVE_BREACH_IOC, MANDIANT_FUSION_IOC and OPEN_SOURCE_INTEL_IOC feeds are deprecated in favour of GTI_IOC, with removal on 18 March 2027. Strategic direction is unchanged — Gemini agents layered across triage, investigation and hunting, with the Emerging Threat Center in public preview mapping new intelligence to detection coverage. Research caution for this vendor: Google SecOps ships continuously through release notes rather than press releases, so the absence of announcement-style news does not mean the absence of change; and the 2022 Chronicle Security Operations launch coverage still ranks highly for "Google SecOps announcement" queries.
