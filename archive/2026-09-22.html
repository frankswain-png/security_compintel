TLDR

This was a rare quiet window across the board: none of the six vendors produced a verifiable product launch, acquisition, partnership or threat-research finding dated between 18 and 21 September. The lull follows an unusually dense run of vendor set-pieces — CrowdStrike's Fal.Con, the Goldman Sachs Communacopia investor stage, Splunk's .conf26 and Mandiant's Cyber Defense Summit all landed in the three weeks prior — and this window itself spans a weekend, when vendor press activity typically drops off. The two standing stories worth tracking are unchanged and simply older: CrowdStrike's FalconFlank privilege-escalation flaw in the Falcon sensor has now gone 18 days without a CVE, a CVSS score or a sensor fix, and Microsoft's ShieldCrash bypass of the Defender ShieldBreak patch (CVE-2026-69414) has gone 13 days without acknowledgement — both remain open, uncatalogued issues rather than new developments this cycle. All six vendors — CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Palo Alto Networks, Microsoft, Splunk and Google SecOps — were quiet on new announcements this window. Watch for SentinelOne's OneCon26 (20–22 October, Las Vegas) and Microsoft Ignite, expected in November, as the next likely sources of competitive news.

CrowdStrike

Quiet

Nothing verifiable landed from CrowdStrike in the 18–21 September window — no product launch, acquisition or partnership dated inside it. The most recent CrowdStrike news predates the window and was already reported: a Leader placement in The Forrester Wave: External Threat Intelligence Service Providers, Q3 2026 (17 September), and Gartner Peer Insights "Voice of the Customer" Customers' Choice recognitions spanning Identity Threat Detection and Response, SIEM, User Authentication and Managed Detection and Response (16 September) — all analyst validation rather than new product news.

The standing story remains FalconFlank, the Falcon sensor privilege-escalation proof-of-concept published 3 September by a researcher operating as Chaotic Eclipse. As of 21 September, 18 days on, CrowdStrike has confirmed no CVE, published no CVSS score and shipped no sensor fix; the only guidance remains disabling the "Microsoft Office File Suspicious Macro Removal" Windows policy in favour of Cloud Anti-malware for Microsoft Office Files. No in-the-wild exploitation has been identified in that time, which keeps this a governance question rather than an active incident.

Watch signal: 18 days without a CVE or fix on a privilege-escalation path in a security agent that runs with elevated trust is now a long enough gap to raise in patch-discipline conversations, independent of CrowdStrike's separate analyst-validation wins. Keep the framing architectural — agent blast radius — rather than campaigning on an unresolved flaw with no confirmed exploitation.

Standing items: Fal.Con 2026 (31 August–3 September) has closed; SafeMind, Falcon Guardian AIDR and Falcon OverWatch for Defender remain the live competitive set from that event. Research caution: Fal.Con and CrowdStrike's Patch Tuesday analyses recur on fixed annual and monthly cadences respectively — confirm the year before treating either as current.

Microsoft

Quiet

Nothing new landed from Microsoft Security in the 18–21 September window — no product announcement, blog post or partnership dated inside it.

The standing story remains ShieldCrash, the Defender privilege-escalation bypass published 8 September, hours after September Patch Tuesday shipped the fix for ShieldBreak (CVE-2026-69414, CVSS 7.8) in the Microsoft Malware Protection Engine. As of 21 September, 13 days on, Microsoft has still not publicly confirmed the bypass, assigned it a separate CVE, or committed to a further Defender update. The proof-of-concept demonstrates an arbitrary file read as SYSTEM rather than a full SYSTEM shell, and no in-the-wild exploitation has been identified.

Watch signal: this is the third disclosure on the same Malware Protection Engine component in four months (RoguePlanet in June, ShieldBreak in August, ShieldCrash in September), with two incomplete patches. The "one vendor, one agent, one console" premise behind Defender XDR plus Sentinel depends on the platform vendor's own remediation holding, and it hasn't on this component three times running — a fair, architecture-first point for independence-of-detection conversations.

Standing items: the Microsoft Defender Threat Intelligence convergence completed its first phase on 1 August, folding standalone MDTI capabilities into Defender and Sentinel at no additional cost. Microsoft Sentinel has been supported only in the Defender portal since July 2026, with the Azure portal experience scheduled to retire 31 March 2027. Research caution: Microsoft Ignite, the usual venue for major Sentinel and Defender XDR announcements, runs in November each year (Ignite 2025 was 17–21 November); any "Ignite 2026" security coverage surfacing before then is last year's event.

Quiet

Watch signal: with .conf26 closed, expect the next wave of Enterprise Security detail to surface as adoption and case-study coverage rather than fresh announcements for the next several weeks — a useful lull for Elastic Security to get ahead of the AWS-native agentic SOC narrative before deal cycles pick it up.

The Splunkie Awards were presented during the .conf26 keynote to Verizon, Global Emancipation Network, Trafikverket, UnitedHealth Group and Anthropic.
